The Business Case for
RAG-Ready Content

Improving documentation structure from "Not Retrievable" to "RAG-Ready" isn't an abstract quality goal: it produces a measurable reduction in support tickets and a calculable return on the documentation investment. This model applies the RAG Readiness Audit Framework to real SAP APM data and produces projected impact numbers any engineering or documentation team can act on.

5.3% Current self-service rate on doc-gap cases
22% Target self-service rate with RAG-ready content
€21.80 Cost per documentation-attributable support case

The self-service rate improvement is determined by the RAG score band change. Moving a chunk from Not Retrievable (0–4) to RAG-Ready (8–10) raises the expected deflection rate from 5.3% (observed baseline) to approximately 22%, a conservative target based on self-service benchmarks for AI assistants with well-structured source content.

Model assumptions: €60,000 annual TCO from ISQM analysis (Jan 2023–Oct 2026, 2,753 cases). Cost per case = €60,000 ÷ 2,753 = €21.80. Baseline self-service rate = 5.3% (ISQM: 4.25% existing solution + 1.05% new KBA). Target rate = 22% (conservative benchmark; not accounting for customer satisfaction or re-open rate reduction). Score bands → estimated self-service rates: Not Retrievable (0–4) → 5.3%; Partially Retrievable (5–7) → 12%; RAG-Ready (8–10) → 22%. All projected figures are estimates. Actual impact depends on content quality, AI assistant configuration, and user adoption.

Prioritising the Work: Impact Per Rewrite Hour

Not all rewrites take the same effort. The right metric is projected savings per hour invested, not savings alone.

The prioritisation formula

Impact score = (Ticket volume × Δ deflection rate × €21.80) ÷ Est. rewrite hours

A pattern with 319 tickets, a 16.7% deflection improvement, and 4 hours of rewrite work scores: (319 × 0.167 × €21.80) ÷ 4 = €289 saved per hour. Use this to rank which content to fix first when time is limited.

What "rewrite hours" means in practice

For a single troubleshooting chunk: 1–2 hours (identify failure mode, research correct resolution steps, rewrite to RAG-ready standard, peer review).

For a full pattern with 3–5 sub-scenarios: 4–8 hours. For a pattern requiring new content creation (coverage gap): 8–16 hours.

At €289/hr impact for Data Synchronization, a 4-hour rewrite investment pays back in under two weeks of ticket volume.

Applying This Model to Any Product

The SAP APM numbers are the proof of concept. The model runs on any product with support ticket data.

1

Get your anchor number

Total annual support cost ÷ total annual cases = cost per case. If your organisation tracks TCO from documentation gaps specifically, use that. If not, estimate: average support engineer cost per hour × average time-to-resolve × case volume.

2

Source your questions

Pull the top 10 ticket patterns from your support system. Derive one concrete user question per pattern. These are your audit inputs. No pattern data? Use search query logs or AI session failure logs instead.

3

Score your content

Run the RAG Readiness Audit Framework against each question's source content. Score 0–10. Record the primary failure pattern. This takes 30–60 minutes per question for an experienced reviewer.

4

Run the model

Apply: (ticket volume × Δ self-service rate × cost per case) for each pattern. Use the score bands to determine Δ self-service rate. Sum across patterns for total projected annual savings. Divide by estimated rewrite hours for impact-per-hour ranking.

5

Present and prioritise

The output is an investment case: X hours of documentation work, Y tickets deflected per year, Z euros recovered. Present to documentation leads, AI engineering teams, or product management. Prioritise by impact-per-hour, not by gut feel.
