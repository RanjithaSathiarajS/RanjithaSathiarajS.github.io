Q2 vs Q3 2026

A quarterly review of four content effectiveness signals for SAP APM documentation: task completion, self-service, helpful sentiment, and support ticket deflection. The Q3 data tells a clear story: visit volume more than doubled, and the content held up.

4 Metrics tracked across 3 data sources

Why These Metrics

Four signals that together give a complete picture of documentation health.

Task Completion Rate (TCR)

Collected via an exit survey on SAP APM Help Portal pages. When users are asked whether they completed their task, the result is a direct measure of documentation usefulness, not page views, not time on page, but whether the content actually helped someone accomplish something.

Source: Task Completion Reporting 1.0 story in SAP Analytics Cloud, filtered for SAP_APM product.

Self-Service Rate (SSR)

The proportion of SAP for Me visits that resolve without opening a support ticket. A customer arrives from the SAP for Me portal looking for help; if they leave without raising a case, that counts as a self-service success. SSR connects documentation quality directly to support cost.

Source: UA Self-Service Reporting Tool 2.0 (SAC), filtered for SAP_APM and visits from SAP for Me.

Helpful Sentiment (HS)

Thumbs-up votes collected via the Help Portal feedback widget. Reported as a raw positive vote count per quarter; total vote counts are not available from the export, so the metric tracks engagement volume and positive signal direction rather than a percentage.

Source: UA Feedback Dashboard, SharePoint export filtered by date range.

Support Tickets Created

The number of support tickets raised immediately following a visit to the APM Help Portal via SAP for Me. Tracking this alongside visit volume shows whether a growth in traffic is creating proportional support pressure, or whether the content is absorbing it.

Source: UA Self-Service Reporting Tool 2.0 (SAC), same dataset as SSR.

What the Data Says

Three things stand out in the Q2 to Q3 movement.

Volume

Visits more than doubled, tickets stayed flat

SAP for Me visits grew 121% from Q2 to Q3 (48 to 106). Over the same period, support tickets raised immediately after a Help Portal visit held at exactly 3, the same number as Q2. The Help Portal absorbed more than twice the traffic without generating proportional escalations. That's a deflection signal, not a coincidence.

Self-Service

SSR improved under pressure

Self-service rate increased from 31.25% to 36.79% (+5.5 pp) even as visit volume more than doubled. With more customers finding the Help Portal through SAP for Me, a declining rate would be the expected result if content quality were not keeping up with demand. Instead the rate rose, evidence that the documentation investment is scaling with traffic.

Watch

TCR dipped: context matters before acting

Task Completion Rate fell from 83.33% to 75.00% (-8.3 pp). The TCR sample is still small: 8 survey responses in Q3 means a single non-completion shifts the rate by more than 10 pp. The dip is worth noting and monitoring, but not yet a signal to act on. If the rate continues downward in Q4 with a larger sample, investigating non-completion reasons (and which topics trigger the survey) becomes the right next step.

How the Data Was Extracted

Each metric comes from a different source and requires a different extraction method.

1

TCR: SAP Analytics Cloud, month filter

Opened the Task Completion Reporting 1.0 SAC story. Applied the Month dimension filter, activating inactive values to surface 2026 months not yet visible by default. Selected Apr, May, Jun for Q2; Jul, Aug for Q3 (Sep was not yet populated at time of extraction). Applied selections using browser event dispatch to bypass a filter token intercepting the Apply button. Read Responses, Completed Tasks, and TCR % directly from the story KPIs.

2

SSR: SAP Analytics Cloud, same filter approach

Opened the UA Self-Service Reporting Tool 2.0 SAC story. Applied the same quarterly month filters. All three Q3 months (Jul, Aug, Sep) were available in the SSR story. Read Visits from SAP for Me, Visits Satisfied, SSR %, and Tickets Created from the story KPIs.

3

HS: SharePoint export, processed with Python

Downloaded the UA Feedback Dashboard export as an Excel file (.xlsx). The export contains thumbs-up-only rows (each row = one positive vote). Q2 positive votes were read from the Q2 export. Q3 votes were extracted from the cumulative Q3 export by filtering for Aug and Sep 2026 rows (the export accumulates from the beginning of the data period). Processed with Python (openpyxl) to count rows per month and isolate the Q3-specific contribution.

Why Quarterly Cadence

Tracking metrics once tells you a number. Tracking them quarterly tells you a story.

Single metrics mislead; change over time reveals

A self-service rate of 36.79% in isolation says very little. Knowing it was 31.25% the previous quarter (and that visit volume doubled in the same period) turns a number into a meaningful signal. Quarterly tracking makes the direction visible, not just the value.

Small samples require interpretation

The TCR sample is a good example: 8 survey responses in Q3 means each individual response carries a 12.5 pp weight. Without tracking the trend and noting the sample size, a single quarter's dip would look like a problem; with context, it reads as a watching brief. Quarterly review builds the context needed to interpret data correctly.
